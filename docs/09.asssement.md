# **4\. Problem Statement**

**Purpose:** AI news is written for adults and mixes hype with fact. Parents of kids ages 5 to 10 want to raise AI literate children, but are too busy at bedtime to find, check, and explain it.

**Our question:** Can an agentic AI system turn real AI news into accurate, age appropriate stories that parents trust and children look forward to every night?

**Our contribution:** A multi-agent pipeline that picks AI news, checks each claim against its source, and lets parents approve stories in seconds. Stories are rewritten for two age levels, read aloud, and end with a fun game.

**Why it matters:** Kids news apps rarely cover AI. AI newsletters are made for adults. Our innovation: agents do the work, parents keep control. Kids who learn AI early will use it wisely and safely.

**Our impact:** AI literate children, and parents who can talk about AI with confidence.

## **4.1. Supporting evidence**

| Claim in the statement | Evidence |
| :---- | :---- |
| Parents want to raise AI literate children | 76% of surveyed US parents want schools to teach responsible AI use (QuestionPro, Oct 2026). Most kindergarten parents surveyed in Hong Kong said it is suitable for their children to learn AI literacy (Education and Information Technologies, 2025). |
| Parents lack simple, safe ways to explain it | 52% of parents and grandparents say they do not feel equipped to help children navigate AI (KidsOutAndAbout, Nov 2025). Nearly half of kids ages 9 to 17 have never talked with a parent about AI safety (Common Sense Media, 2026). |

## **4.2. Competitive analysis**

Competitors were mapped across all four product layers, news, story, narration, and gamification which cover 13 apps across four categories: kids news (Newsela, News-O-Matic, TIME for Kids, BBC Newsround), bedtime story apps (Moshi, Calm, Bedtime Stories AI), AI literacy tools (AI4K12, Machine Learning for Kids, Scratch), and gamified learning (Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo ABC, Epic\!).

We found no direct competitor. Lullabyte sits at the intersection of all four categories, a space no single product occupies. Key findings:

* News apps (Newsela, BBC Newsround) cover current events but are human-written, not age-split into narratives, and have no gamification.  
* Bedtime story apps (Moshi, Calm) produce high-quality narrated stories but are fictional and static, the same content every night, never tied to real events.  
* AI literacy tools (AI4K12, MIT Day of AI) all target ages 10+ and require active screen time, not a passive bedtime habit.  
* Gamified learning apps (Epic\!, Khan Academy Kids) offer the strongest model for streaks and badges but cover no AI news content.

Lullabyte is the only product combining verified AI news, age-split narrative generation, illustrated bedtime format, and gamification for children under 10\. The in-story screen timer and real-world badge rewards are features no competitor has.

## **5.1. Scout: News sources → daily news**

Owner: Deokhwa Jeong   
Pair: Sally Chou   
Code: https\://github.com/sem-baby-cmu/goodnight-ai-news/tree/main/scout

Verdict: feasible. On Nov 4 to 6, the 4 feeds gave 7 to 18 unique stories a day, full text was fetched for 10 of 10 articles, and the LLM marked 43 of 43 claims correctly, including 3 planted false ones. Open risks: the claim check was a self-check by one model, and many days are led by politics or finance news that is not kid-safe.

### **(1) What this component does**

Pulls AI news from several sources every day, removes duplicates, checks each claim against its source, and gives every story a confidence score and a first green / yellow / red rating.  
**(2) Datasets**

| Dataset | Type | Fields we need | Access |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| TechCrunch AI | RSS | Title, summary, date, link | Available now, no key |
| MIT Technology Review AI | RSS | Same, latest 10 stories | Available now, no key |
| The Verge AI | RSS | Same | Available now, no key |
| OpenAI News | RSS | Official announcements | Available now, no key |

**Do the datasets have the attributes we need?** Partly. The 4 RSS feeds give title, short summary, date, and link, but no full text, no images, and no "safe for kids" flag. The OpenAI News feed returns its full archive (1,359 items on Nov 4), so Scout filters stories by date.

### **(4) AI method**

| What we try | What happened |
| :---- | :---- |
| Model or service | Muse Spark 1.3, run in the Muse agent; no separate API call |
| Prompt versions | S3: write 4 claims per story from the full text, check each against the source with a verbatim quote, then plant 1 false claim in 3 stories. S4: 3 wordings (plain support, strict fact-check editor, kids' app trust). S5: one prompt with the green / yellow / red rules. |
| Quality | 10 of 10 stories with all claims marked correctly (43 of 43 claims) |

Multiple datasets: merge 4 feeds and count how many stories appear in more than one source. Expected insight: the same story from several outlets is itself a trust signal. Result (Nov 4 to 6): 5 events appeared in 2 feeds (Nov 4: 0, Nov 5: 3, Nov 6: 2), none in 3 or more. ### **(5) Risks**

| Risk | Did it happen? | How we handle it |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Scraper blocked by news sites | No. 4 of 4 feeds and 10 of 10 articles returned HTTP 200\. One OpenAI timeout, fixed by a retry with a 60-second timeout. | Fallback: Guardian API; retry with a longer timeout |
| No AI news on some days | No for the 4 feeds combined (7, 18, 13 stories). Yes for single feeds: 3 feed-days with 0 stories. | Use all 4 feeds; use stories from the last 3 days |
| LLM marks an unsupported claim as verified (biggest risk) | No. 0 of 43 wrong, 3 of 3 planted claims caught. Weak test: the same model wrote and checked the claims. | Require a quote from the source for every claim; add an independent checker (a second model or a human spot-check) |
| Free API delays news by 12 hours | No. Lag to the newest item: TechCrunch 0.8 h, The Verge 1.4 h, MIT 9.6 h, OpenAI 11.6 h. | Acceptable for a bedtime product |
| Green / yellow / red rules are unclear, even for people | Yes. Two human labelers agreed on only 14 of 20 stories (kappa 0.54). All 6 splits were green or yellow calls on money, work, or technical stories | Write clearer rules for money, work, and technical stories, then relabel. Until then, send any story where the two labelers or Scout disagree to yellow (parent review) |

### **(6) Discoveries**

1\. RSS summaries are too thin to write from (average 1.8 sentences, 33 words), so Scout must fetch full text.

2\. Article length varies a lot: The Verge 196 to 384 words, TechCrunch 577 to 1,318, MIT 842 to 1,417. Short articles carry fewer checkable claims.

3\. Dedup by link found 0 duplicates, but dedup by event found 6\. Scout must match titles or events, not just links. Cross-feed overlap does happen (5 events in 3 days), so it can work as a weak trust signal.

4\. The news was led by the 2026 midterm election, finance, and hacking. Only 9 of 20 rated stories were green, so Scout must pull enough stories each day to find 3 that are kid-safe.

5\. The OpenAI feed returns its full archive (1,368 items) with times set to midnight PT, so date filtering works by day, not by hour.

6\. Method note: the day 1 count of 27 used a rolling 24-hour window. The counts above use calendar days (PT).

7\. Hand labels (Sally and Judy, 20 stories): the two humans agreed on only 14 of 20 (kappa 0.54, moderate), so the rating rules are unclear even for people. All 6 disagreements were "green or yellow" calls on money, work, or hard-to-explain tech stories. On the 14 agreed stories, Scout matched 12, missed 2 by rating a business story green, and never rated green a story the humans rated red. Scout matched 4 of 6 full-text stories and 8 of 8 RSS-only stories, so the next fix is clearer rating rules, not more text.

## **5.2. Storyteller: Daily news → age appropriate scripts**

**Owner:** Sally Chou  
**Pair:** Judy Lee  
Code: [https\://github.com/sem-baby-cmu/goodnight-ai-news/tree/main/storyteller](https://github.com/sem-baby-cmu/goodnight-ai-news/tree/main/storyteller)

Verdict: feasible. In a 3-story pilot (6 scripts), the LLM followed the 10-page structure 6 of 6 times and hit the reading level 5 of 6 times after one prompt revision. The open risk is subtle fact drift (added motives and adjectives), which our automatic check does not catch. Hand labels are done: Scout's first rating matches the final human label on 16 of 20 stories (80%), with 0 unsafe misses, but the two human labelers first agreed on only 14 of 20\.

### **(1) What this component does**

Takes one fact-checked story from Scout and rewrites it into two bedtime scripts, one for ages 5 to 7 and one for ages 8 to 10, using a fixed 10-page structure. Every sentence carries a tag (True / Maybe / Imagine / Explain), and a Real World Facts page quotes the source word for word. It also returns a parent card, 3 key facts for Playmate, a scene description and tone tag per page for Producer, and a first green / yellow / red rating. Every story starts in Draft status.

*Ideas carried: \#2, \#4, \#10, \#11 \+ \#14 (merged into one parent card), \#17, \#18, \#19. Out of scope this round: \#20 review workflow and \#21 review checklist. Storyteller only sets review\_status \= draft and the rating; the workflow belongs to the parent view.*

### **(2) Datasets**

| Dataset | Type | Fields we need | Access |

| Hand-labeled ratings | Spreadsheet | 20 stories, green / yellow / red, 2 labelers | [hand\_labeled\_ratings](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1rtfxV4exslRM0cXiTVchcy9MzWIyzHz_MOCQuo5Q5FA/edit?gid=916704859#gid=916704859) Done: both labelers 20 of 20; the 6 disagreements were settled by discussion |

### **(6) Discoveries**

1. **The real hallucination risk is drift, not invention.** The LLM never made up a name or number. It added *why* ("to stay safe") and *what kind* ("simple apps"). These sound harmless but are not in the source, and a keyword check cannot see them.  
2. **Three tags were not enough.** Kids' stories need definitions ("A hacker breaks into computers they should not use") and tips ("Keep your passwords secret"). Neither is fact from the source nor fiction, so we added `explain`.  
3. **Reading level is set by the topic, not just the writing.** The same prompt scored grade 1.1 on the AI-helper story and 2.5 on the hacker story for ages 5–7, because the true words are long.  
4. **Names push the reading level up.** Removing company and person names from 5–7 story pages (keeping them on the facts page) was the single biggest fix.  
5. **Our tools needed testing too.** The auto checker raised 6 false alarms (title words, a word inside a quote, a possessive), and the syllable counter miscounted words like "files" and "saves." We fixed both and added a 25-word self-test (25/25 correct). On \~100-word scripts, the grade moves about ±0.5 with the counter, so treat the 2.0 and 4.0 cutoffs as bands.  
6. **The output must carry more than the script.** Producer needs a scene description and a tone tag on every page, and Playmate needs exactly 3 key facts. Putting them in the schema now avoids a handoff mismatch later.  
7. **Scout's misses are all on the lenient side, never the unsafe side.** Against the final human labels, Scout matches 16 of 20 (80%) with 0 unsafe misses. All 4 misses are stories Scout rated green that people rated yellow: 2 funding deals, a paywalled work-agent story, and a hard math story. 3 of the 4 are in stories 1 to 10, where Scout read the full article (70% agreement, against 90% on summary-only stories). With 10 stories per group, that gap is not yet meaningful.  
8. **People disagreed more than we expected.** Sally and Judy first agreed on only 14 of 20 stories (kappa 0.54). Every split was green vs yellow, and Sally was the more lenient labeler on all 6: a shopping helper, a security model, a finance tool, a paywalled work-agent story, sponsored security content, and a hard math story. The rubric needs worked examples for money, paywalled, and hard-tech stories before labels can serve as ground truth.

### **(1) What this component does**

Turns one bedtime story script into every format it is published in.

## **5.4. Playmate: Story → interaction and gamification**

**Owner:** Madhuri Dusad  
**Pair:** Jinyu Liu

The Playmate component has working code, a mock dataset with all required fields, multi-story cross-dataset analysis, and documented prompt engineering discoveries across four AI features (P1–P4).

### **(1) What this component does**

Playmate is the engagement and safety layer that runs after every story. It delivers two age-specific interactive games, Story Scene Tap for ages 5 to 7, where children tap an illustrated scene to find hidden story elements with no wrong answers, and True or False Zapper for ages 8 to 10, where children swipe statements left or right to build critical thinking about AI news.

Every story ends with a Word Flip Card, one new AI word per day, with a fun illustration on the front and a simple definition on the back. Words are saved to the child's personal collection. A child who reads every day from age 5 to 10 will learn 1,825 AI words by their 11th birthday.

A floating Ask the AI bubble sits on every page for both age groups, letting children ask any question about the story and get a friendly, age-appropriate answer.

Badges reward reading milestones at 5, 10, 25, 50, and 100 stories. Each milestone unlocks a real-world reward suggestion like a park trip, a game zone outing, or a family activity delivered by the parent, not the app.

A built-in 10-minute screen time timer starts when the story begins. At 8 minutes a gentle moon animation appears. At 10 minutes the story pauses at the end of the current page.

Parents can extend by 5 minutes once per night. All progress like streaks, badges, word collection, and age level are saved on the device using localStorage. No child account, no sign-up, and no personal data is ever collected, keeping the product fully COPPA compliant.

### **(6) Discoveries**

**Prototyping code**: Built an interactive browser demo (playmate.html) covering all 5 features with no API key required, plus a batch test runner (run\_tests.py) across 10 mock AI stories testing 4 hypotheses (P1–P4).

**Dataset attributes**: Playmate requires 6 fields from the Storyteller output: key\_facts\[3\], hidden\_elements\[\], ai\_word, script\_5to7\[\]/script\_8to10\[\], age\_level, and parent\_card. All fields were confirmed and sufficient in mock\_input.json. No missing attributes were found.

**Multi-dataset integration:** Running prompts across 10 stories covering different AI topics (medical, transportation, art, security, weather) revealed that abstract AI words (Chain-of-thought, Anomaly detection) require more careful definition framing than concrete words (Agricultural robot, AI tutor). Stories tagged 5 to 7 needed shorter, single-clause key facts to avoid run-on quiz statements. This cross-story pattern was not visible from a single story test.

**ML/AI algorithm discoveries:**

Adding "Grade 4 reading level" to the True/False prompt eliminated technical jargon (words like "neural network") that appeared in v1 without that constraint.

Without a source constraint, the LLM added plausible but unstated facts to quiz questions. Requiring use only the key facts listed" fixed hallucinated additions across all 10 stories.

Specifying exactly one FALSE statement per quiz was necessary, without it, the model sometimes generated all-true quizzes, which have no learning value.

The Ask the AI safety deflection required an exact phrase ("Let us talk about tonight's story\!") specified in the prompt. Open-ended deflection instructions produced inconsistent phrasing that broke automated safety testing. With the exact phrase, 30/30 unsafe questions were deflected correctly.

# **6\. Architecture Design**

This section shows how the five components run together as one product on Cloudflare (revision 2, November 8, 2026). Each story runs as one Workflow. Every stage output is a versioned row in D1. People review each story at three gates. The source of truth is `integration/architecture.md`.

## **6.1. Flow**

![][image4]

1. A daily cron starts the Scout job. Scout writes news items to D1 (`news_item`) and stops.  
2. Gate A selects one news item, by a person or by an auto rule. This creates one StoryWorkflow instance, and the instance ID is the `story_id`.  
3. Storyteller writes the script. Automatic checks run on it: schema, FK grade, and claims.  
4. Gate B: a person reviews the script. Reject with notes starts a new Storyteller round.  
5. Producer makes the scenes (SVG), then the voice and word timings.  
6. Playmate generation makes the quiz and the word card.  
7. Packager builds `story_package` version N.  
8. Gate C: a person approves the story for children. Regenerate with notes goes back to the named stage.  
9. Publish. The reader and API serve the package.

A gate is `step.waitForEvent` with a 14 day timeout, and the reviewer page sends the event. Each step reads its inputs by ID and writes one new version: artifact rows and `story_package` in D1, and SVG, MP3, and timings in R2. Producer and Playmate generation run one after the other, so nothing has to wait for two branches.

## **6.2. Decisions**

| \# | Decision | Why |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| D1 | Workers Paid. Free is for a demo only. | Free gives 10 ms CPU per invocation and 50 external subrequests. One story needs more (see 6.6). |
| D2 | Scout is its own cron job. It writes news items and stops. | Scout can run, fail, or run again without touching any story. |
| D3 | One Workflow instance per story. The instance ID is the story ID. | The platform retries and checkpoints each step. A crash resumes at the failed step. |
| D4 | Each stage output is an artifact row in D1. Steps pass IDs, not content. | D1 rows do not expire. Workflow state is kept 7 to 30 days after the instance ends. |
| D5 | Human gates are `waitForEvent` with an explicit timeout. | A waiting instance does not count toward the concurrency limit. |
| D6 | The front end reads one versioned story package that never changes. | A published story never changes under a reader. |
| D7 | UUID v7 for every ID, story\_id included. A date is an attribute, never an ID. | Ordered by time and unique across stages. The schema enforces it. |
| D8 | No Queues and no KV in the first release. | The Workflow gives retry and ordering. Status is a D1 column. |

## **6.3. Data model (D1)**

| Table | Columns | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| news\_item | id, run\_id, source\_url, source\_title, full\_text, claims, confidence, first\_rating, status, created\_at | Scout output. Status: new, selected, skipped. |
| story | id, news\_item\_id, publish\_date, status, published\_package\_id, created\_at | `publish_date` is an attribute, not a key. |
| artifact | id, story\_id, stage, version, generation\_id, input\_ids, input\_hash, payload or r2\_prefix, author, model, prompt\_version, cost\_usd, checks, status, notes, created\_at | Unique on (story\_id, stage, version) and on `generation_id`. |
| review | id, artifact\_id, reviewer, action, notes, created\_at | Action: approve, reject, edit, regenerate. |
| story\_package | id, story\_id, version, manifest, input\_ids, created\_at, approved\_by, approved\_at | Never edited. A change is version N+1. |

Retries: `generation_id` is the instance ID plus the step name plus the loop round. A retry reuses it, so the insert does nothing and the step reads the first row. A regeneration is a new round, so it gets a new version even with the same inputs.

## **6.4. Quality: automatic checks and human actions**

| Point | Check | On failure |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Gate A | `first_rating` is green; confidence is 4 or higher; source is on the allow list | The auto rule skips it. A reviewer can still pick it. |
| After Storyteller | Schema; FK grade per age; every claim is in the source with a quote; allowed tone | Retry once with the reasons in the prompt, then show the failure at Gate B. |
| After Producer | Every page has a scene; audio length within 10% of the estimate; timing words match the text | Platform step retry, then show at Gate C. |
| After Playmate generation | Quiz answers appear in the script; no new facts | Retry once, then show at Gate C. |
| Packager | Every input is approved or passed; every R2 key exists | The step fails and nothing is published. |

| Action | Gate | Effect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| approve | B, C | The Workflow continues. |
| reject \+ notes | B | New Storyteller round with the notes in the prompt. |
| edit | B | The edit is saved as a new version with `author = human`. The Workflow continues from it. |
| regenerate \+ notes | C | Loop back to the named stage. Every later stage runs again. The script stays if only Producer is regenerated. |

| Stage | Runtime | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Scout | Worker cron handler | RSS fetch; one model call per item through AI Gateway. |
| Storyteller | Workflow step | Model calls through AI Gateway. Waiting on a model is not CPU time. |

| Producer feed PNG | Browser Run | Only the 6 carousel cards need a raster image. |
| Producer voice | Workflow step | TTS and forced alignment through AI Gateway. MP3 and timings go to R2. |

The vertical reel was dropped on November 8, 2026, so nothing needs ffmpeg or Containers. The prototypes are in Python; each owner ports their stage to TypeScript, because Python Workers run on Pyodide without Playwright or Pillow.
